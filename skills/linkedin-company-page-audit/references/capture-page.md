# Capturing a Company Page for Audit

Company pages are public by default, which makes the logged-out read reliable
and honest: it is exactly what a prospect sees before they trust you.

## Rules

- Never log in as the user. No credentials, no session cookies.
- One page at a time. No bulk collection, no competitor list scraping. Checking
  two or three named competitors as part of one audit is fine; harvesting is not.
- If an auth wall or challenge appears, stop and ask for a paste.

## Procedure

1. Open `linkedin.com/company/<slug>/` logged out.
2. Capture: name, tagline, About text, industry, size, HQ, founded, website,
   specialties, follower count, custom button label.
3. Open the `/posts/` tab and record the last 5 posts: date, format, topic, and
   visible reaction and comment counts.
4. Check `/people/` for how many employees list the company. A page with 8
   employees and 3 listed has a free fix sitting there.
5. Check for a Products or Services tab and whether it is filled.
6. Screenshot the top of the page for banner, logo, and above-the-fold layout.

## What is not public

Page analytics (visitors, unique visitors, button clicks, follower
demographics), lead-gen form data, and competitor analytics. Ask the user for
these from the page Analytics tab; `linkedin-metrics-review` needs them.

## Competitor comparison

When comparing 2–3 named competitors, hold the comparison to what matters:
tagline clarity, post cadence, content mix, and whether their proof is visible.
Follower count comparisons are mostly noise for small businesses, since a page
that bought or inherited followers converts nothing.
