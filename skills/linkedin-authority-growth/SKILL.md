# LinkedIn Authority Growth

Build the kind of credibility that makes people pre-sold before the first call.
Authority is a byproduct of visible, specific competence repeated in public. It is
not a personal-brand aesthetic.

Use this after `linkedin-profile-audit` (the asset) and alongside
`linkedin-content-strategy` (the output engine). This skill covers position, proof,
and network: the parts that make the same content land harder.

## Pick the depth

- **Quick:** the user asks one thing ("help me narrow my niche", "what should my
  signature series be"). Run that step only and deliver its output.
- **Full** (default): Steps 1 to 6.

## Step 1: Claim a specific position

Broad expertise is invisible. Narrow expertise travels. Run the narrowing test with
the user, for example:

| Too broad | Better | Ownable |
|---|---|---|
| Digital marketing | Ecommerce growth | Store speed and checkout conversion for D2C brands on WooCommerce |
| Coaching | Leadership coaching | First-time engineering managers at Series A startups |

Ask: if someone in the audience had this problem at 11pm, would the user's name
come to mind? If not, the position is still too broad.

Test the position against three checks:

1. Can you say it in 8 words?
2. Does it exclude most people? (It must.)
3. Do you have proof in it, or can you build some within 90 days?

Narrowing does not shrink the business. It makes referrals possible, because people
can only refer what they can describe.

## Step 2: Build the proof stack

Credibility ranks in a strict order. Move the user up it deliberately.

| Tier | Proof | How to get it in 90 days |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your own results, documented | Publish build logs, numbers, and screenshots as you work |
| 2 | Client results, named | Ask 3 past clients for a result you may post; offer to draft it, and they approve the wording |
| 3 | Volume claims | "47 stores audited": count what you have already done |
| 4 | Third-party validation | Podcast guest spot, article, community talk, tool-partner listing |
| 5 | Original research or a free tool | A survey of 100 people in the niche, or a small calculator |
| 6 | Recommendations on the profile | Ask 5 people directly, give one first, suggest what to mention |

Never invent or inflate any of it (`references/voice-rules.md`). One checkable claim
outperforms five vague superlatives, and a caught exaggeration ends the exercise.

## Step 3: Grow the right network

Followers are only useful if they are the audience. Deliberate network building
beats hoping for reach.

**Comment strategy, the highest-leverage growth activity that is not posting:**

- Build a list of 15–25 accounts the buyers already read: peers, adjacent experts,
  and a few large accounts in the niche. **The user names them.** You may suggest
  categories and ways to find them (who comments on the user's own posts, who the
  buyers follow, LinkedIn search for the niche sorted by recent posts). Never invent
  names, handles, or follower counts. If a browsing tool lets you confirm a public
  profile exists, link it; otherwise write "verify before engaging".
- Comment within the first 30 minutes of their post, 2–4 sentences, adding a
  distinct point or an example. Never "Great post!".
- 10 a day. Expect profile visits and follows within a few weeks. Comments count
  toward the daily action ceiling (`references/platform-reality.md`), so they
  replace other activity, not add to it.
- Treat a comment as content, not a courtesy. It is the cheapest reach on the
  platform: it puts the user's name in front of an audience they did not have to
  build.

**Collaborations that compound:**

- Guest on niche podcasts and community AMAs. Small and specific beats large and
  general.
- Co-write a teardown with a peer who serves the same audience from a different
  angle.
- Newsletter or community swaps.
- Publicly credit other people's work. Reciprocity is the whole mechanism.

**Connections vs followers:** make Follow the primary profile button once the
audience outgrows the network, but keep accepting connection requests from the
target audience: a connection can message the user directly and sits in their
first-degree network, which a follower does not.

## Step 4: Own a repeatable format

Authority accelerates when people recognize the work on sight. Pick one signature
format and run it weekly for 12 weeks:

- A numbered teardown series ("Teardown #14")
- A weekly numbers post from real work
- A "what I changed this week" build log
- A recurring myth-bust for the niche

Consistency of format is what makes a stranger's third exposure feel like
familiarity.

## Step 5: Set a 90-day arc

Give dated phases, not a vague ambition.

- **Days 1–30, credibility base:** profile fixed, 5 proof posts published, 3
  recommendations requested, comment routine started.
- **Days 31–60, visibility:** signature format running weekly, 2 collaborations
  booked, one lead magnet live.
- **Days 61–90, compounding:** one piece of original research or a free tool, a
  first podcast or guest appearance, inbound DMs tracked weekly.

Set one honest expectation up front: meaningful authority takes 6–12 months of
consistent output. Anyone promising 30 days is selling engagement pods.

## Step 6: Deliver

1. The narrowed position in 8 words or fewer, plus what it deliberately excludes
2. Proof stack audit: the tier they are on, and the next three proof assets
3. Target account list: the names the user supplied, organized by category, plus
   search methods to fill any gaps (no invented names)
4. The signature format, with the first 4 installments outlined
5. The 90-day arc with dated milestones
6. Metrics: followers from the target audience, profile views, inbound DMs, and
   unsolicited referrals, with today's baseline (or "not provided")

## Anti-patterns

- Engagement pods and comment automation. LinkedIn treats coordinated and automated
  engagement as low quality, and the engagement is worthless anyway.
- Buying followers or connections.
- Broad motivational content to farm reach. It builds an audience of nobody.
- "Personal branding" as aesthetics: logos, brand colors, a tagline, no proof.
- Fake scarcity, fake client counts, invented case studies, invented target accounts.
- Claiming thought leadership. Demonstrate the thinking and let others say it.
