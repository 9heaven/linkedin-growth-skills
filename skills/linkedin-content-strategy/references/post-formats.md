# Post Formats and Templates

Structures, not scripts. Fill them with the user's real material in the user's own
words. Every one of these fails if the content underneath is generic.

## 1. Result teardown (proof pillar)

```
[Specific before -> after number, one line]

[One line of context: who, what kind of business]

What was actually wrong:
1. [problem]
2. [problem]
3. [problem]

What we changed:
- [change, and the effect]
- [change, and the effect]
- [change, and the effect]

[The counterintuitive part: what did NOT matter]

[One takeaway the reader can apply today]
```

## 2. Contrarian point of view (authority pillar)

```
[The belief everyone holds, stated fairly in one line]

That is wrong, and here is what it costs you.

[2-3 lines on the damage it does]

What actually works:
[The alternative, concretely]

[Why the common belief persists anyway]

[A genuine invitation to disagree]
```

Steelman the opposing view first. Cheap contrarianism reads as posturing.

## 3. How-to or checklist (teaching pillar)

```
[The outcome, as a promise with a constraint: "in 20 minutes", "without paid tools"]

Step 1: [action]
[one line of why]

Step 2: [action]
[one line of why]

Step 3: [action]
[one line of why]

[The step people skip, and what breaks when they do]

[The deeper asset: link in the first comment, or "want the full checklist? It's in the comments"]
```

## 4. Failure story (personal pillar)

```
[The loss, stated plainly and specifically. No hedging.]

[What you were thinking at the time]

[The moment it became obvious]

[What you do differently now, concretely]

[No moral-of-the-story flourish. Stop when the story stops.]
```

## 5. Document carousel (8–12 slides)

- Slide 1: the promise, in 6 words or fewer, huge type
- Slide 2: who it is for, and the problem
- Slides 3–9: one idea per slide, one visual per slide, no paragraphs
- Slide 10: the summary, screenshot-able
- Slide 11: who you are in one line, plus the CTA

Design for a phone: 1080 x 1350 (4:5), 40pt or larger type, high contrast. Export
as PDF.

## 6. Native video (30–90 seconds)

- First 3 seconds: the claim, spoken, no intro
- Middle: one point, one example
- End: one question to the viewer
- Burn in captions. Most people watch muted.

## 7. Poll

For audience research only. LinkedIn shows fewer polls from people members don't
know, so a poll is not a reach tool. Four options at most, one of them genuinely surprising. Post the
results a week later as a follow-up with your interpretation: the follow-up is
worth more than the poll.

## 8. Offer post (1 in every 8–10)

```
[Who it is for, specifically]

[The problem, in their words]

[What you do, in 3 lines]

[Proof: 1-2 real results]

[What it costs, or the honest range]

[Who it is NOT for]

[Exactly how to start]
```

Stating the price and the disqualifier is what separates this from an ad.

## Lead-magnet mechanics

"Comment [WORD] and I'll send it" works, but LinkedIn reduces the reach of posts
that ask for engagement mainly to boost reach. Use it rarely, only for an asset
worth the ask, and always offer the plain alternative: the link in the first
comment.

## Cross-cutting rules

- 900–1,300 characters for text posts: long enough to be worth the click, short
  enough to finish.
- The first two lines are 80% of the work. Write them last, after the post is
  drafted.
- One idea per post. A post that makes three points makes none.
- On company pages, links always go in the first comment. On personal profiles, the
  first comment is the safe default when reach matters.
- Reply to every comment in the first hour, with a sentence that adds something.
