# Pillar Worksheet

Extract content pillars from what the user already knows. Ask these questions
in order and write down the raw answers; the pillars fall out of them.

## Extraction questions

**Authority / POV pillar**
1. What does almost everyone in your niche believe that you think is wrong?
2. What do you refuse to do for clients, and why?
3. What advice do you give that people resist at first?
4. What changed your mind in the last two years?

**Proof pillar**
5. What is the best result you have produced, with numbers?
6. What is the most common problem you fix, and what does fixing it look like?
7. What did a client's situation look like before and after?
8. How many of these have you done? What have you seen that others have not?

**Teaching pillar**
9. What do clients get wrong before they hire you?
10. What could someone do themselves in 30 minutes that would help them?
11. What is your actual process, step by step?
12. What tools or checks do you run every time?

**Personal pillar**
13. Why do you do this specific work?
14. What was the expensive mistake you learned from?
15. What are you building or testing right now?

## Turning answers into pillars

Group the answers into 3–4 themes. Name each pillar in the user's own language,
not in marketing categories. "Speed is a revenue problem" beats "Technical SEO".

For each pillar write:

- **The sentence:** what this pillar argues, in one line.
- **The audience state:** what the reader believes before, and after.
- **10 topic seeds:** each specific enough to write in 20 minutes.
- **The proof it draws on:** which items from the proof inventory.

## Quality check on a pillar

- Could the user write 30 posts in it without repeating themselves? If not, it is
  a topic, not a pillar.
- Does the buyer care, or only peers? Peer-only pillars grow the wrong audience.
- Does at least one pillar make some people disagree? If everything is agreeable,
  nothing will travel.

## Refresh cadence

Revisit pillars quarterly, using `linkedin-metrics-review` data: keep the pillar
that produced conversations, cut or narrow the one that produced only reactions.
